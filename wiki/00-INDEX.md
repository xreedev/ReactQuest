## The one-paragraph summary

The job description is a procurement document; the interview is a JavaScript-fundamentals exam wearing a React costume. At these companies the technical filter is usually **one 45–60 minute round** covering JavaScript + React + a little HTML/CSS + a small coding task + your project — so **breadth of ready answers beats depth in any one area**, which is the opposite of product-company advice. Candidates over-invest in the JD's vocabulary (cloud, CI/CD, testing, micro-frontends) and under-invest in closures, the event loop, and being able to explain *why this component re-rendered*.

## How to use this wiki

1. Read `03` first — it tells you what to ignore, which is worth more than another list of topics.
2. Work `07` as your schedule.
3. Use `02` and `04` as reference, not as reading material.
4. Do `08` + `09` + `10` in a single weekend; they affect interview *volume*, which is usually the actual bottleneck.
5. Re-read `06` after any rejection, before changing your study plan.

## Confidence note

Everything here is tagged by evidence strength in `12-sources.md`. In short:
- **Strong:** what gets asked (many independent candidate reports converge)
- **Moderate:** process structure and timelines (Glassdoor aggregates, self-selected samples)
- **Weak:** frequency claims, any percentage, and named ATS products (vendor-sourced)

Self-reported interview writeups are self-selected. Nothing here should be treated as a measured statistic unless the source is named as a dataset.
